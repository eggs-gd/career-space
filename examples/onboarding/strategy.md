# My job search strategy

Not just "find the highest employer-side fit" — `playbooks/fitment.md` already answers "can I
clear their bar." This is the other half: what's actually worth my time. See
`playbooks/prioritize.md`.

Fictional example candidate (Alex Morgan, matches `config.yaml`/`sources.yaml` in this same
folder) — shows the shape and tone this file should have, not a strategy worth copying.

## My tracks, and what they're actually for

- **Backend Developer (primary)** — this is the real target. I want a normal senior IC role:
  clear ownership of real systems, a codebase I can actually improve, no need for constant
  hand-holding either direction.
- **Full-stack (fallback)** — genuinely fine with this, but only when the stack overlaps enough
  that I'm not relearning a frontend framework from scratch on day one. A full-stack role at a
  company I'm otherwise excited about is worth it even without a perfect stack match — see the
  exception below.

I am not pursuing Engineering Manager or any people-management track right now (see
`config.yaml`'s `rejected_broader_titles`) — this isn't a gap to be talked into, it's a deliberate
choice. Don't let a role's "IC path to EM" framing read as a point in its favor.

## Preferences (weigh these, don't gate on them)

Usually attractive:
- Remote, or Berlin-based on-site/hybrid.
- Small-to-mid team where my own changes are visibly felt, not lost in a large org.
- A codebase that's actively maintained and evolving, not legacy-preservation mode.
- Postgres-centric backends, or close enough that the evidence transfers cleanly.

Usually unattractive:
- Pure maintenance/keep-the-lights-on roles with no room to improve anything structural.
- Heavy on-call rotation as the role's main selling point.
- A stack far enough from Python/TypeScript that most of my evidence wouldn't transfer.

## Hard constraints

(Only what isn't already mechanically enforced elsewhere — see `sources.yaml`'s
`local_keywords`/`hard_exclude`, already gate location/dealbreaker phrases at fetch time.)

- Comp floor: below my current base is a hard no, not a "worth discussing" — regardless of how
  good the fit score looks.

## Exceptions — when to ignore the above

- **A full-stack role is worth pursuing even with a weaker stack match if the company itself is
  unusually strong** — a product I'd genuinely want to work on, or a team with a reputation worth
  the ramp-up cost. Don't discard a fallback-track role solely on stack mismatch without checking
  this first.
- **A slightly-below-band comp offer is still worth a look if the role would meaningfully
  strengthen my story for the next jump** (e.g. genuine system-design ownership I don't have
  direct evidence for yet) — the hard comp floor above is about a role with nothing else going
  for it, not this case.
