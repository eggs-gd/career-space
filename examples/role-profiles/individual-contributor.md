# CV lens: individual contributor

Title: **Senior Backend Developer / Software Engineer**

For: Software Engineer, Backend Engineer, Full-stack Engineer, Senior Software Engineer, Product
Engineer, Platform Engineer roles where the ask is direct delivery on a scoped problem, not
organizational or technical-direction ownership.

Primary focus: taking a ticket, bug report, or ambiguous request through to working, shipped
code; fixing defects; improving reliability, performance, and maintainability; working inside an
existing codebase and existing team process rather than redesigning either; collaborating with
product/QA/other engineers as a peer contributor, not as the person accountable for the team's
overall output.

Evidence selection:
- Prefer the technically specific version of an achievement (the actual bug, the actual endpoint,
  the actual query) over the aggregate/organizational framing of the same event.
- Prefer bullets that stand on their own as a self-contained piece of technical work, not ones
  that only make sense with organizational context (who reported to whom, how many teams were
  coordinated).
- Leadership shows up only as code review, mentoring one or two people, or technical influence —
  never as the organizing structure of the story.
- Don't reach for scale/scope language ("led," "owned across teams") when the underlying evidence
  is one person doing one piece of technical work well.

Positioning:
- Position as a reliable engineer who takes scoped problems from ticket or ambiguity to working
  software — not someone managing other people's work.
- Never invent ownership of product strategy, architecture direction, hiring, budgets, or
  cross-team mandates unless the Master CV explicitly supports it.
- Keep the tone practical and delivery-oriented: what shipped, what improved, what became easier
  to maintain or operate.
- Don't over-optimize for seniority language. A strong IC CV reads credible because the work
  described is specific, not because every bullet is dressed up to sound like a platform
  strategy.

Experience emphasis: expand the most recent hands-on implementation roles to 2-3 bullets; compress
any role that was primarily about managing people or coordinating other teams down to its direct
technical contribution, if it has one; keep older roles only when they show relevant
implementation depth, domain familiarity, or durable engineering habits.

Summary: describe the recurring kinds of technical problems the candidate solves; support with 2-4
concrete proof points about shipped work, code quality, reliability, or collaboration; never open
with a leadership or organizational claim.

Skills groups, exactly these, in this order:
- Backend Engineering & APIs
- Quality, Reliability & Delivery
- Collaboration & Technical Communication
